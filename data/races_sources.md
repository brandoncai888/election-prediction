# 2026 race roster provenance

Verified October 21, 2026. The seed covers **506 contests**: all 435 voting US House seats, 33 regularly scheduled Class II US Senate seats, the Florida and Ohio Class III Senate special elections, and 36 state governor elections.

Coverage excludes nonvoting delegates, territories, state legislatures, local offices, ballot measures, primaries as separate races, and House special elections for the remainder of the 119th Congress. Candidates are not seeded: incumbency alone does not establish candidacy or ballot qualification. This is a roster of seats for the upcoming midterm cycle, not a complete record of every election held during calendar year 2026.

## Primary sources

- **House seat counts**: [US Census Bureau, 2020 Census apportionment results](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2020/dec/2020-apportionment-data.html), [Table 1 PDF](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/decennial/2020/data/apportionment/apportionment-2020-table01.pdf). The 50 state counts sum to 435. The six single-district states use the Census at-large district code `00`: Alaska, Delaware, North Dakota, South Dakota, Vermont, Wyoming. The roster does not use obsolete 2010-apportioned counts.
- **Federal election day**: [Federal Election Commission, 2026 Congressional Primary Dates](https://www.fec.gov/documents/5910/2026pdates.pdf), which identifies November 3, 2026 as the federal general election day. The FEC notes that schedules can change and directs election-administration questions to state election officials.
- **Regular Senate seats**: [US Senate, Class II seats whose terms expire in 2027](https://www.senate.gov/senators/Class_II.htm), cross-checked against the [current Senate roster and class column](https://www.senate.gov/senators/). The 33 Class II seats are regular elections for terms starting January 3, 2027; they are distinct from the two Class III specials.
- **Florida Senate special**: [Florida Division of Elections, Offices Up for Election and Retention in 2026](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/candidates-committees/offices-up-for-election) identifies one Senate seat on the 2026 ballot. The [Senate roster](https://www.senate.gov/senators/) identifies the appointed seat as Class III. [Florida Statutes section 100.031](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/Chapter100/All) provides for vacancies to be filled for their unexpired terms at the general election, and the [Florida election calendar](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/for-voters/election-dates) dates that election November 3. This seat's unexpired term ends January 3, 2029.
- **Ohio Senate special**: [Ohio Secretary of State Directive 2025-54](https://www.ohiosos.gov/globalassets/elections/directives/2025/directive-2025-54---writ-of-special-election-for-united-states-senate-election.pdf) explicitly sets the special general election for November 3, 2026 and the end of the unexpired term at January 3, 2029.
- **Governors**: [National Governors Association, 2026 gubernatorial elections](https://www.nga.org/governors/elections/) lists the 36 state contests and 3 territorial contests. Only the 36 states are included here. This avoids mixing territories, which have different demographic-data availability, into the initial state/federal database.
- **Louisiana schedule exception**: [Louisiana Secretary of State, Types of Elections](https://www.sos.la.gov/elections-voting/types-of-elections) and [FEC election activity dates, note 5](https://www.fec.gov/help-candidates-and-committees/dates-and-deadlines/2026-reporting-dates/federal-election-activity-periods-each-state-2026/) identify the November 3 House election and a December 12, 2026 runoff if no candidate receives more than 50 percent. The database stores the first decisive election date, November 3, and describes the possible runoff in the Louisiana House notes; it does not duplicate a seat into a second forecast race.

## Geography caveat

`state:06` is a statewide lookup identifier. `cd:0601` combines state FIPS `06` and congressional district `01`; `cd:0200` means Alaska at-large. These identifiers do **not** by themselves encode a district map vintage. Every House row therefore carries an explicit boundary warning. ACS 2024 congressional district statistics generally describe the 119th Congress, while the November 2026 election is for the 120th Congress and can use a different map. Before calling any congressional forecast production-ready, confirm the 2026 election map and use a block/tract geographic crosswalk or population reaggregation where the ACS boundary differs. A district number surviving redistricting does not prove that its population has stayed the same.

The roster intentionally avoids labeling any district map as verified for 2026. Post-census apportionment fixes the number of seats per state but does not fix the boundaries inside a state.
