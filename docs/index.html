HOW I CHOSE MY PREDICTIONS

Picks that maximize expected points

A correct pick scores log½(p) points, where p is Kalshi's price for the candidate I pick; a wrong pick scores 0. Calling a 97% favorite earns 0.04 points, while calling a 3% long shot earns 5.1, so the model's favorite is not always the best pick. Each House and Senate race is decided by these rules:

    How the forecast works

    Projections for all 435 House seats, 35 Senate contests and 36 governor races.

    1. Who lives there. Census data splits each state or district into 32 groups of adults 25+ by race/ethnicity, sex and education.
    2. How they voted in 2024. A penalized logistic model fitted to the 2024 Cooperative Election Study estimates each group's Democratic share, then shifts all groups together so each race matches its actual 2024 presidential result.
    3. National environment. The 2026 national House vote is half the average out-party midterm gain since 2010 and half the generic-ballot polling average. Groups that historically swing more in midterms move more.
    4. Midterm turnout. Groups are weighted by their validated 2018 and 2022 turnout rates, so each midterm electorate differs from its 2024 one.
    5. Group news. Recent coverage of specific racial groups and their issues shifts those groups further. The size of that effect is fitted to the 2010–2022 midterms.
    6. Margin to odds. Groups add up to a projected margin. Win probability is Φ(margin / σ), with σ combining race-level error (7 pts House and Senate, 9 governor) and 5 pts of national error. Chamber control comes from 20,000 simulations.
    Research limitations
    • Demographic associations do not establish individual voting behavior.
